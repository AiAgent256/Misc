# Stormhold

Private Forge 1.20.1 mod for the Dungeons and Homestead pack. Castles held by rival houses, sieges, capture, and armies you command afterward.

## Playing it

**Finding castles.** Stormhold generates three tiers of its own castles (watchtowers, holdfasts, great castles) in five regional styles chosen by biome: Northern grey stone (taiga, snow), Riverlands brick and timber (plains, forest, swamp), Reach white stone with green copper roofs (flower fields, meadows, cherry groves), Dornish sandstone (desert, badlands, savanna), Stormlands blackstone (dark forest). Castles from When Dungeons Arise, IDAS, Illager Invasion and Dungeons and Taverns are garrisoned too (see config). `/stormhold nearest` names the closest known castle.

**The garrison.** Knights patrol the baileys, archers and crossbowmen hold the walls, a lord sits in the great hall. They wear Epic Knights armour and carry shields in their house's colours, and with Epic Knights installed they fight with its weapons: knights a shortsword, katzbalger, mace or morningstar beside the shield, captains a bastard sword, lords a flame-bladed sword, in iron, diamond or netherite by renown. The Armoury issues the same. Attack one and the whole castle comes for you.

**Siege rules while a house holds the castle.**
- Walls can't be broken or built on. Ladders, scaffolding, torches and TNT are allowed.
- Portcullises, doors, glass and iron bars can be broken (pickaxe, TNT, or Siege Weapons rams).
- Explosions only break those siege-breakable blocks.
- Chests stay sealed until the lord is dead.

**Taking it.**
1. Kill the lord. At half health he calls his household guard.
2. Carry a War Banner (crafted: any banner + iron ingot + stick; sneak-use in the air to change colour) to the banner stand at the top of the keep and use it on the stand.
3. The garrison counterattacks. Hold the stand with no defenders within 12 blocks for 90 seconds. A bar shows progress.
4. The castle is yours: banners turn your colour, half a garrison swears to you, and a War Table appears in the hall.

**Commanding (War Table).** Lists every castle you hold.
- *Defend*: faster reinforcement, stronger against raids.
- *Produce*: makes goods every day (bread, iron, gold, arrows, emeralds at higher tiers). Surplus goes to the supply target by courier.
- *Attack*: sends a war party to the target coordinates every few minutes. They file out of the gate, march, fight for five minutes, and come home. A party you watch that can't find its way (walls, water, cliffs) carries on as a count and reappears further down the road. If the target is a house castle whose lord is dead, they can take it for you.
- *Scout*: two riders from the garrison ride to the x and z in the target boxes (up to 5000 blocks, 8 blocks a second there and back), look over the country for 500 blocks round it and come home with every castle, town and city there: who holds it, about how many men, how its house stands with you, and the ground where castles nobody has seen yet will stand. What they saw goes on the Realm page. Houses that hate you and the far frontier can cost a rider; lose both and there's no report. One party out per castle.
- *Supply to*: any chest, barrel, or modded inventory (MineColonies huts work), or another castle you hold. Sneak-use your War Banner on a chest to mark it as a depot and the table pre-fills it. Couriers walk the road and can be ambushed by brigands.
- *Company*: pick how many knights, archers and crossbowmen you want (shift-click for fives) and *Muster*. They walk out of the garrison and fall in beside you; each castle keeps at least one of each. *Dismiss* sends every man of yours within 24 blocks into that castle's garrison. Both need you within 128 blocks of the castle. With Villager Recruits installed your company is Recruits units (use its follow/guard orders); without it they're Stormhold soldiers who follow you, catch up if you ride off, and go after whatever attacks you.

- *Armoury*: *Iron*, *Diamond* or *Netherite* gives a full set of armour and a weapon for the role (sword, bow or crossbow) to every man of yours within 24 blocks who's wearing worse: 26 iron, 26 diamonds, or 26 diamonds + 5 netherite ingots per man, paid from your pack first and then the castle's stores when you're within 128 blocks of it. *Enchanted* adds Protection and Sharpness/Power (II iron, III diamond, IV netherite), Unbreaking III, fire and piercing on netherite, for 10 experience levels per man. Old kit comes back to you. Works on Recruits units too.

**Your company.** Men you muster, call by column or hire at a city are Stormhold soldiers sworn to you (set `companyUsesRecruits` in the config to make them Villager Recruits units instead). They follow you, keep up with a horse, and go after whatever hits you or whatever you hit.
- *Marshal's Baton* (stick + gold ingot + red dye). Point and use it: at a creature or enemy soldier, every man attacks it; at a block, they clear the building around it (14 blocks across, 7 up and down), tell you when it's clear and fall back in; sneak at a block, they hold that spot; at the sky, switch between Follow (they only fight what's close to you) and Aggressive (anything hostile within 24 blocks); sneak at the sky, fall in. Hit one of your own men with it and he's told apart: while the company follows you he holds where he stands, while it holds a spot he comes with you (leave four at the cave mouth, take the rest down). Hit him again and he rejoins them; any order for the whole company calls everyone back in.
- *Kit*: right-click one of your men with an empty hand to open his kit: helmet, chest, legs, boots, main hand, off hand and a three-slot food pouch. Right-click him holding armour or a weapon to put it straight on; his old piece comes to you. Sneak-right-click for his health and orders. If he dies, everything he carried drops.
- *Care*: out of a fight they heal slowly; below half health they eat from the pouch. Each man takes three bread from the realm's stores when he joins. Right-click a man with food or a potion to heal him yourself (if he's whole it goes in his pouch). His health shows after his name.
- *Getting there*: following men use ladders, vines, ropes and scaffolding to reach you, and if there's no way on foot (a cliff, water, a wall) they find one after a few seconds and appear at your side. Walk through a portal and every man within 48 blocks who was following comes through after you; columns you've called find you in whatever world you're in. Men told to hold or clear stay put.
- Your men and the guards of a house that counts you a friend leave each other alone; hitting a friendly guard with your men costs standing as if you'd done it yourself.

**War Horn.** A War Table you carry: War Banner + gold ingot + 4 copper ingots. Sound it anywhere to open the same screen, so you can call your men out of a castle as you ride past it and send them home when you're done.

**Work you can watch.** The fief's miners drive a real tunnel out from the bottom of the mine shaft: two wide, three tall, ramping down to working depth, with timber frames, a rail and lanterns, turning every eighteen blocks. It advances a block every fifteen seconds while you're within 96 blocks and a dozen more each day; everything they cut goes into the stores (stone as cobblestone, ores already smelted), so the mine feeds the Master Builder. Pastures breed: a pair or two have young each day, with hearts, until the pen holds twelve; past that the butcher takes the commonest grown beasts for meat and leather, and the sheep are shorn. Ripe crops in the fields are cut and replanted at dawn. The census line on the War Table shows how deep the mine has gone.

**The realm.** Every castle you hold is one realm. Each keeps its own stores, but anything that spends from them (the Armoury, the Master Builder, raising fief buildings, training the garrison, a siege tower) spends from that castle first and then from the others, so a quarry in one place pays for walls in another. The War Table shows the castle's stock and the realm's total under it. Couriers and supply lines still carry goods to real chests.

**Growth.** A castle that is fed (16 bread in the realm) and at peace (no raid in the last day) grows on its own: every three such days the fief raises another building, up to three for a watchtower, nine for a holdfast, fifteen for a great castle; every twelve the Master Builder adds a stage of fortification if the stone is there; the garrison trains up to capacity from the stores all the while, and villagers breed into the new beds. A raid sets the clock back.

**The siege tower.** Sneak-use the War Horn on open ground and your men raise a siege tower six blocks ahead of you: a timber tower on wheels, ladders up the inside, a drawbridge at wall height, and its own War Table on the ground floor, so the whole command screen (and every column you call) is at the foot of the wall. 48 planks and 8 iron from your pack, then the realm.

**The Master Builder.** Once a castle is yours, a Master Builder stands at the War Table. *Repair walls* has him put back every block of the castle's plans that's missing (the gate you broke, the portcullis, the holes from your TNT), bottom up, in the castle's own style; a stone from the stores per block keeps the pace, without stone the men scavenge and go slowly; your own additions are left alone. *Fortify* adds defences in three stages, each costing stone and iron from the stores (48/6, 96/12, 144/18) and letting the castle hold 15% more men: a palisade of sharpened stakes seven blocks out from the walls, then a moat outside that, then timber hoardings with lanterns over every archer's post. The road out of the gate is left as a causeway. Three projects sit beside them (click the project's name to pick, *Go* to start): *Light* has him walk every floor inside the walls and set a torch wherever a monster could spawn, as few as will do and on the walls where he can, a coal or charcoal from the stores per four torches (crypts and cellars stay dark); *Clear* sends woodcutters to fell every wild tree within 24 blocks of the walls, leaves and all, logs into the stores, leaving orchards, fief buildings and anything in a town alone; *Road* lays a road with lamps from the gate to the nearest place you hold that hasn't one yet (up to 3000 blocks), a stone per ten blocks, painted as the land it crosses loads.

**Every gate answers.** The gate guard watches every portcullis the castle's plans record, not just the ones by the front gate: the inner ward's gate in the square castle, the shell keep's in the ring fortress, the keep enclosure's in the castle-town, both inner gates in the cities. An inner gate whose chunks weren't loaded when the castle was first seen is picked up a few seconds later.

**Gates open to travellers.** A house's portcullis raises for anyone it has no quarrel with, and for that traveller's own soldiers: you start wary with every house, and wary is let in. Only a player the house holds hostile, or one its men are fighting at the gate, finds it down (and a castle you own opens for you alone). A monster or a hostile army within seven blocks of a portcullis keeps it shut; stand at one that won't rise and you're told who's holding it and where. Sheathe nothing, just walk up. Land inside the walls is the castle's: any hill the siting allowed is cleared from the floor to the top of the template before the castle is set down, so no bank of earth hangs in the yard and no sand pours into the doorways.

**Room to live.** The small castles grew. Watchtowers are 48 blocks across instead of 36: the round one has a wall-walk behind its parapet reached by two stairs and a 21-wide tower, the timber one a fighting platform behind stakes five high and an 18-wide peel tower, and both have a barracks, a stable, a well and a smith's bench in the yard. Holdfasts are 80 and 72 across instead of 64 and 56, with bigger keeps, a second lodging, a smithy and a well. Castles already standing keep their old plan; the masons and the gate guard check the plan's size before touching a castle, so a redrawn plan is never built over an old one.

**The scale audit.** Every build is now measured as well as walked. Over the cells a soldier can reach, the generator counts stair treads with nothing beside them (a one-wide flight), one-wide cells with open floor at both ends (a doorway or gap the widener missed), the alleys a building leaves against a wall, and rooms without a light; a lamplighter hangs a lantern in every dark room first. The small castles are held at zero narrow stairs and dark rooms and at most three pinches; the big plans carry the backlog the first run found (quay stairs in the cities, a few gaps on their walks) as their limits, so anything new fails the build and the old ones wait their turn. Spirals are two wide by construction and are counted, not measured.

**Two more plans.** A third holdfast, a ringwork: a round curtain with four towers and drum-towered gate, a round keep twenty-three across in the middle with the hall below and the lord's floor above, barracks, lodging, stable, smithy and well round the yard, radial stairs up to the walk. A third watchtower, a motte and bailey: an earth mound six high with a fourteen-square tower on top, a stair cut into its face, a palisaded bailey with a fighting platform below. Each comes in two builds, so six watchtower and six holdfast plans now. The cities' donjons climb by a stair hall and their inner-wall towers are fourteen square.

**Stairs a man would climb.** Keeps and square towers are climbed by a stair hall now, not a corkscrew: two straight flights three wide with a half landing between them, one dog-leg per storey (a quarter landing part way up a tall storey), a spine wall between the flights, rails along the drop on every floor and on the roof. The watchtower peel, both holdfast keeps and the great castles' keeps have one; round towers keep their two-wide spirals, and the straight stairs up to a holdfast's wall-walk are two wide. A lord trades with anyone his house doesn't hold hostile, and so do the city's house sellers; only hiring soldiers at the Muster Post still wants friendship. The first-sight guide now speaks for every castle, not only cities and towns.

**The great castles, roomier.** Their towers grew where the footprint allowed: corner towers 23 across with the stair turret moved out to the corner so the spiral below can run two wide, flanking and gatehouse towers a block wider all round, the mid-wall towers 16 square and set further out over the moat, the inner gatehouse towers 13 wide, the ring fortress's eight curtain towers 19 across and its donjon 25, the castle-town's corner towers 16 square with open porches to the yard. Doors through tower walls now cut to the room whatever the radius, and corner-tower doors land on the curtain walk wherever the curtain meets the tower.

**The masons.** A house's castle mends itself. A dragon's fire, a blast at the gate, a broken portcullis: once the castle has been quiet for a minute the house's masons set to work and put every block back the plans call for, bottom up, and say so to anyone within earshot. (Your own castles still repair at your word, through the War Table.) And castles now keep their distance from one another: a hundred blocks of open ground at least between any two, and fewer of them, so a great castle is a day's ride from the next and a watchtower stands alone on its hill.

**Houses and standing.** Every house has an opinion of you, from sworn enemy to sworn friend. About a third start friendly. `/stormhold houses` lists them. Any house that doesn't hold you hostile opens its gate, lets you walk its yards, and its lord sells bread (an emerald) or iron (sneak + emerald); its guards turn on you only if you strike first. A friendly house also hires you soldiers, never raids you, and sends knights when someone else does. A hostile one shuts its gate and attacks on sight. If its lord is dead you can take the castle by bending the knee: raise your banner and the garrison swears to you at once, no counterattack. You earn standing by killing monsters at their gates and running supply lines to their castles. You lose it by hitting their guards, marching on them, or taking their castles. Standing drifts back toward the house's nature one point a day.

**Your own holding.** Build a castle or town, sneak-use a War Banner on two opposite corners (12 to 192 blocks a side), then place a War Table inside. Stormhold scans your walls for guard posts and the place becomes a holding you own: empty at first, trained up from bread and iron you put on the War Table (use the table while holding supplies), raided by the local house if it dislikes you, and commanded like any captured castle.

**The Great City.** Once per house region (about every 1500 blocks) a capital generates: 256 blocks across, three terraces climbing to the keep, harbour, sept, market with a notice board, barracks, Dragonpit. It is the seat of the house that rules the region and starts friendly: walk in, trade with its king (an emerald buys bread, sneak for iron), use it as a base. Its garrison is 80 strong but only the district you're in is populated at any time. Take it (kill the king, raise your banner, hold the donjon) and every leaderless castle of that house bends the knee to you.

**Miners and smiths.** In a castle you hold, the War Table's Works row sets the castle's own people to the mine and the forge: click Miners or Smiths for one more, shift-click for one fewer, up to eight of each. The chosen commoners take the trade, their name over their heads ("Ada the Miner"); shopkeepers and the Master Builder are never taken. Each dawn every miner brings up a day's ore into the stores, smelted: 6 coal, 3 iron, 2 copper, and by chance gold, redstone, lapis, emeralds and diamonds, more diamonds the farther out the castle stands, and ancient debris from zone 7. Smiths forge the garrison's kit a level above what the castle has: 24 iron for levels 2 and 3, 8 iron and 12 diamonds for 4 to 6, 8 diamonds and 4 netherite ingots for 7 to 9, taken from the stores when the work starts, then six smith-days (three smiths, two days). When a level is done every man on the walls is re-armed at it and every man after comes kitted at it, up to netherite Protection IV. The tooltips show yesterday's haul and the forge's progress.

**The steward.** Put a sign on a chest or barrel inside a castle or town you hold (on its side, or standing on top) and the steward takes it as a standing order; he says so in chat when he takes charge. The first line is the order: *Sell* sells everything a shop would buy at the shops' price and leaves the emeralds in the chest; *Stores* empties it into the castle's stores (for the builder, the smiths, the armoury); *Send to Wenton* (or *Send* with the place on the next line) has a courier carry it to that place's stores; *Keep* with lines like `64 bread` or `arrows` keeps the chest topped up from the realm's stores, which is also how you take things out of the stores; *Buy* does the same and buys what the stores lack at the shops, with the chest's emeralds first and then the stores'. Add `when full` to Sell, Stores or Send to wait until the chest has no empty slot, or `over 64` to leave that many of each thing. Enchanted gear, named things and anything else carrying data are left alone. He looks every ten seconds while you're near, and the morning account says what each steward did.

**Pets and friendly soldiers.** Your tamed dragon, wolf or horse and the soldiers who aren't your enemies can't hurt each other: a dragon's fire across a melee or an arrow through the press does nothing, so nobody turns on anybody. That covers your own garrisons, your company, your Recruits units and every house that doesn't hold you hostile. A house that does is fair game both ways. Castles attack only wild dragons on sight.

**The frontier.** The farther you ride from the frontier's centre, the harder the world. The centre is world spawn until you stand where you want it and type `/stormhold frontier here` (free once; after that it takes an operator). Every 2,500 blocks out is a zone harder, nine in all, from the Heartland to the World's Edge at 20,000; `/stormhold frontier` tells you where you are, and crossing into a new zone says so. A castle's people are kitted at its renown or its zone, whichever is higher: diamond in the Borderlands, netherite knights from zone 7, netherite with Protection IV on every knight at the edge (vanilla netherite, since Epic Knights makes no netherite plate). Castle and town markets carry the frontier's goods: enchanted diamond from zone 4, netherite from 6, Protection IV netherite, Mending, totems and enchanted golden apples out at the edge. And every hostile mob, bosses included, joins the world as hard as its zone: up to three times the health, twice the damage, eight more armour, and the armed kinds in iron, diamond or netherite. The Nether counts eight to one; the End counts out from its middle, ten to one. All of it is in the `frontier` section of the config.

**Shops.** Every town and city keeps its traders in their shops: a keeper stands behind each counter and stall all day, his name and trade over his head ("Hob the Armourer"), the shop's name on its sign. There are eleven shops: the General Store (tools, torches, beds, maps, seeds, wool, everyday goods), Food & Provisions, the Armoury (leather to diamond, priced like a master villager's), the Weaponsmith (vanilla and Epic Knights arms), the Smithy (tools and metalwork), the Fletcher, the Enchanter (lapis, bookshelves, an enchanting table, anvils and enchanted books), the Alchemist (brewing and potions), the Mason & Carpenter (stone, brick, planks, glass), Stables & Tack (saddles, horse armour, name tags) and the castles' Quartermaster. The older plans' trades fold into these: their keepers take the new trade, stock and sign the first time you're there. Prices run a fifth under a master villager's, in three bands: one to three emeralds for what you use up, four to twelve for tools and armour, sixteen and up for enchanted and frontier gear. Every town has an Enchanter: one whose plans had no room for it puts him at a spare counter. Every shop buys the goods of its trade, the valuable ones dear: diamonds (5 each), blaze rods, ghast tears, ender pearls, netherite scrap, saddles and horse armour, melons and mob drops by the stack. Each restocks every morning. Regulars pay less: every six trades at a place's shops takes another percent off, up to 25%. A keeper never stands in a doorway; one who was moves behind his counter. A keeper killed is replaced the next morning. A house that holds you hostile won't trade. The notice board in the market (right-click any of its signs) lists every shop with the way to its nearest counter, the homes for sale and the work on offer. Market towns sell some of their homes too, 18 emeralds, the capital's 24. Every great castle keeps a yard market too: a Quartermaster (bread, beef, arrows, torches, healing potions, a spyglass, a saddle), an Armoury, a Weaponsmith and a Fletcher, each under a coloured awning on open ground in the yard, off the road in from the gate and off the muster ground. The castle puts its stalls up the first time it's seen with all its ground loaded, so castles already standing get them as well; the castle guide points the way. Towns and cities standing in a world from before shops get them the first time you're there: their keepers, labelled board and homes for sale come from today's plans, and where an older build put a counter a step off, the keeper stands beside it.

**Finding your way.** The first time you come within sight of a city or a town you're told what's there and which way it lies from where you stand: the gate, the king or reeve, the Muster Post, the Notice Board, the nearest house for sale, the inn and the smithy, each with a bearing and coordinates, plus where you stand with the house and what that lets you do. `/stormhold guide` says it again for the castle or town you're in or nearest.

**Living in the city.** At the barracks, use the Muster Post holding emeralds: 6 hires a knight, sneak for an archer (5), sneak with an empty off-hand for a crossbowman (5). They join your Villager Recruits company. Any door marked for sale in the city (use it to see the price, 24 emeralds) buys you the house: its chests open for you, you can build inside, and a bed there is your bed in the capital. The Notice Board in the market square lists jobs (clear a bandit camp, break a raider warband 200 to 300 blocks out); click one in chat to take it, kill them, and the city pays emeralds and standing. All of this needs the city's house to like you, or the city to be yours.

**The Realm page.** *Realm* on the War Table (or `/stormhold realm`) opens the map: every castle you've come within sight of, drawn in its house's colour with a border for how that house stands with you (white yours, green friendly, grey wary, red hostile), a crown on a house's seat, a red flag on a castle under attack, a black dot where the lord is dead. Armies on the march are drawn as lines. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, hover for the details. Beside it: your holdings (men, folk, builder, days to the next raid, stores), the houses you've met (standing, castles, who they're at war with), and the record.

**The Chronicle.** Everything that changes the map is written down: wars, castles changing hands, envoys, what the fiefs raise. At dawn every lord gets the day's account in chat: the realm's stores, who marches where, what grew, offers still open. `/stormhold chronicle` reads it again.

**The houses act.** Every few days each house does one thing of its own. Rival houses (about a third of all pairs start that way, and every war makes it worse) march on each other's castles: if you're there it's fought at the walls, if not it's settled by numbers, and the castle changes hands, new lord and banners and all. A house raises a building at one of its fiefs. Or an envoy comes to a lord it likes with an offer: so many emeralds for a rival's castle within eight days, paid the moment you take it, with standing on top. The enemy of your enemy thinks better of you when you take his rival's castle. `housesAct` in the config turns it off.

**Dragons.** With Ice and Fire installed, a wild dragon that comes within a hundred blocks of a castle is the castle's business: the men on the walls shoot at it, the knights go for it when it lands, and the dragon is given the garrison to fight rather than the fief's sheep. You're warned in chat if you're within sight or it's your castle. A dragon slain at a castle raises that castle's renown by one and, if you killed it, the house's standing with you by 30. Every dragon a garrison sees is written down, and the Realm page marks it: a red diamond where it was last seen, dim once it's dead, hover for how long ago. A house with a dragon loose within 800 blocks of one of its castles puts a price on its head (60 emeralds plus the castle's renown and size, ten days to collect) and tells every lord it doesn't hate; the first to kill it is paid on the spot and the house's standing rises by 20. A dragon slain at a castle also leaves its skull mounted in the great hall beside the lord's seat, sized to the beast. `dragonsAttackCastles` in the config turns the fighting off. (Stone walls survive dragonfire when Ice and Fire's `dragonGriefing` is set to 1; roofs and timber burn and the Master Builder puts them back.)

**Market towns.** Between the castles, on flat ground, the houses' towns: a walled hill town with one gate, a crossroads town with a toll bar, a village round a green with a pond. Each has a stone church with a spire and a bell (the town watch's archers stand on its tower), a market hall where the reeve sits over an open arcade with the War Table and the strongbox, a market with stalls, an inn, a smithy, a tithe barn, a well, and terraces of party-wall houses with shops on the ground floor, in the same style as the Great City's streets. A town belongs to the house of its region, is held by a reeve rather than a lord, and keeps a watch of a dozen men. Take it like any castle (its banner stand is on the church tower) and the market pays you a tithe every day: emeralds, bread and wheat into the stores, whatever stance it's in. Houses raid each other's towns as they do castles; a house that loses its towns loses the men and income that came with them. `/locate structure #stormhold:town`.

**Matters for the lord.** Some mornings, with the dawn account, the steward brings a matter: blight in the fields, a smith at the gate with a cart of iron, deserters, an envoy under a white banner, a tourney invitation, rats in the cellar, brigands on the road, a mason with an offer, a wedding, a town's petition against the tolls, a spy caught in the kitchens, a relic dug up under the keep. Each has two answers, clickable in chat, and both cost something: bread or emeralds now, or men, folk, standing or renown. Take two days over it and it settles itself the way unattended things do, and the Chronicle says so. One matter at a time, about one morning in three. `/stormhold matters` shows the open one again; `events` in the config turns the deck off.

**Crypts.** Under every holdfast, great castle and city keep there is a crypt: a ladder down from a hatch in a ground-floor room (three low walls round it so nobody walks into it in the dark), then a vaulted hall of tombs along both walls with candles on the lids, alcoves of skulls between them, an iron gate, and at the far end the founder's tomb on a dais between the house's banners with the family's treasure in a chest behind it. The temper colours it: soul fire and chains for a grim house, candles everywhere for a pious one, gold on the founder's lid for a rich one. The castle's floor over the crypt is laid in dark stone, which is how you find the hatch.

**The Canal City.** Capitals now come in two plans. The Great City has the harbour on its south wall; the Canal City lets the harbour in: the water passes under the south wall through a twenty-block arch between two drum towers (a portcullis down to the water on either side) and runs as a canal up the middle of the lower city to a dock basin at the foot of the grand stair, with quays and a crane at the head, warehouses with hoists on the water, jetties, ladders down to the boats, statues of admirals along the quay, and three stone bridges high enough for a boat to pass under. The market is split either side of the water. The upper city and the keep are the same bones. Which plan a region's seat gets is the dice.

**The Nether holdfast.** Holdfasts generate in the Nether too, in their own palette: polished blackstone and basalt for the walls, nether brick where the north uses deepslate, crimson timber, soul lanterns and soul torches, red glass, warped roots in the yard and magma where a well would be. They are sited on the floor of the Nether, on a ledge above the lava with headroom under the roof, and held by the house of their region like any castle; there is no fief outside the walls (nobody farms down there) and no roads. `/locate structure #stormhold:nether_holdfast` from the Nether.

**Built for men, not blocks.** A man is not a block wide. Every tower with the room for it has a 7x7 stair shaft with treads two wide (24 steps a turn, a gentler climb, and a landing you can step onto from a door on any side); only the smallest turrets keep the one-wide spiral. Every doorway one block wide, in a castle, a town house or a city street, is opened to two wide and three tall, with double doors where there was a door. The walkability audit checks all of it afterwards.

**Titles and the cost of a crown.** The realm names you by what you hold: Knight with one castle, Lord with three, Warden with a great castle, King with a city. A Knight musters ten men at a time, a Lord twenty, a Warden forty, a King eighty, and a King takes two emeralds a day in tribute from every friendly house. Taking a castle scales with the castle: a watchtower only has its counterattack, a holdfast sends one more wave of the garrison at the stand while you hold it, a great castle three, and the city five with the king's guard last, so the big ones take an army, not a lone swordsman. Raids come one at a time: while one of your castles is under attack, the others' raids wait.

**Renown.** Every castle has a renown from 1 (a poor place) to 5 (a famed seat), rolled from its size, how far it is from spawn, and luck. You're told it in chat as you come within sight, and `/stormhold info` shows it. Renown sets the garrison's kit: chain at 1, iron at 2, Protection I at 3, diamond with Protection II at 4, diamond Protection IV with Sharpness III and Unbreaking at 5, netherite lords. Soldiers get 12% more health per rank. It also sets the payout: after the lord falls, the first chests opened in the keep carry extra treasure, from a golden apple at 2 up to two Protection IV pieces, a Sharpness V sword, netherite, an enchanted golden apple, Mending and a totem at 5. Watchtowers cap at 3, holdfasts at 4; the Great City is always 5.

**Monsters.** Every garrison treats hostile mobs as intruders: knights cut down anything that gets inside the walls (they leave creepers to the archers), archers shoot whatever comes in range.

**Fighting the garrison.** Knights reach as far as you do, three blocks in any direction, so standing on a ledge two blocks up doesn't make you safe. Knights use ladders, vines and scaffolding to get at you, including the siege ladders you leave on the wall. A knight who takes hits from someone he can't reach for a few seconds backs off out of reach and leaves you to the archers. Archers work their stretch of wall: if the shot is blocked or too long they move along the battlements to a spot that can see you, and if nothing on the wall can (you're inside, under them) they come down for a while and fight in the yard. Get up on the wall next to one and he hits you with the bow.

**The gate.** The portcullis is a solid iron grille: break it (it's tough), climb, or blast it. The gate guard raises it for the castle's own men when they're going somewhere (marching out, chasing someone, riding with you, carrying goods) and for players the house likes, or for you at a castle you hold, and drops it again behind them. It never raises while an enemy is within a few blocks of it, so the garrison can only sortie once you've backed off. Raiders held at your gate for most of a minute bring up a ram and the portcullis comes down until the raid is over; the next time the castle's chunks load after that, the garrison has it back up. Every castle has one, watchtowers included (a grille between the gate pillars, or across the palisade gap), and castles generated before their template had a gate get one fitted the first time you come near. A gate left up with nobody about comes down on its own. In creative the gate opens for you like for any friend. Find castles with `/locate structure #stormhold:great_city` (or `#stormhold:great_castle`, `#stormhold:holdfast`, `#stormhold:watchtower`, `#stormhold:castle` for any of them). The templates are only read when a chunk actually places them, so `/locate` and loading chunks near a city cost nothing.

**The Great City is packed.** Inside the walls the lower city is party-wall terraces of tall narrow houses, two to four storeys, jettied out over lanes a cart wide, with shopfronts on the ground floor (counters, awnings, goods, hanging signs) and living above, dormers in the roofs, chimneys smoking, washing lines across light-well courts, covered passages and timber bridges between houses. Trades cluster by street: a market street of bakers and chandlers, Smiths' Row, the harbour quarter of fishmongers and tanners, and a market square ringed by arcades with stall clusters inside it. Walls everywhere now carry buttresses, blind arcading, putlog holes and weathering from foot to parapet; buildings get quoins, sills, lintels, shutters, door hoods, torches and chimney caps.

**Every post can be walked to.** The generator runs a walkability audit on every castle: starting at the gate it walks like a soldier would (two blocks of headroom, one-block steps, doors, ladders, open trapdoors) and refuses to export a castle with a post it can't reach. Stair shafts get flat landings at every floor, tower doors go through the whole wall, furnishing keeps out of passages and stairwells, and a last pass opens any sill or plinth that runs across a doorway.

**Castles claim their ground.** Other mods' surface structures (villages, towns, towers, camps, dungeon entrances) give way wherever a castle could stand, so nothing generates on top of a castle or inside its walls; castles in turn keep clear of villages and each other. `castlesClaimGround` in the config turns it off.

**Alarms.** A garrison turns on whoever strikes one of its men, for half a minute, and on nobody else: a monster hitting a guard sets the guards on the monster, and a friendly visitor standing next to them is left alone.

**Raids.** Every 3 to 5 days the house you took the castle from marches to retake it. You get a warning a day ahead. If you're away it's fought out by numbers; Defend stance helps.

**Illager warbands.** Every 5 to 9 days an illager warband comes for each castle, town or city you hold, the way barbarians come for a border fort. Scouts warn you a day ahead; horns sound when they come over the hills. A captain carries the banner; behind him pillagers and vindicators, a witch or two, and for a holdfast or bigger evokers and a ravager (two for a city or far out). With Illager Invasion installed, bashers, provokers and, the farther out, sorcerers, inquisitors and invokers. The band grows with the place and with the frontier zone. They don't just stand at the walls. The ravager goes for the gate and staves in the portcullis (without one, axes hack through it after a minute). Held at the walls for 45 seconds, they raise ladders up the outside face at two places and send men up and over. Held for 90, sappers set a charge under the wall and blow a breach (`warbandSappers` in the config turns that off). Inside, they go for your men and the banner. Clear the yard of your garrison and hold it 20 seconds and they sack the place: a quarter of the emeralds, diamonds, gold, iron and bread, and a third of the garrison. The castle stays yours. Kill them all and their spoils, an emerald a head, go into its stores. If you're away it's fought out by numbers (Defend stance helps; the frontier makes them harder). Only one fight at a time for a lord: a house raid or another warband pushes it back a day. `illagerWarbands`, `warbandMinDays` and `warbandMaxDays` in the config.

**The fief.** Nobody lives in an empty castle. As the chunks around a castle load, a settlement of commoners is laid out on the land outside the walls, sized by the castle: a watchtower gets a hamlet (three cottages, a well, a pasture), a holdfast a village (five cottages, a fenced pasture with cattle and sheep, a mine head with a shaft and ore, a windmill, a chapel), a great castle a town (all that plus a lumber yard, a quarry, a fishing dock if there's water, and eight cottages). Each building comes with a family or a crew (a soul a bed and a couple more), the castle itself keeps a household of six a tier, towns and cities a crowd, the pens with their herds, and a knight's beat at the door. Once a day the castle takes a census, and if you hold it the stores fill from what the living workers make: wheat and bread from farmers, wool and mutton from shepherds, meat and leather from the herds, iron and coal from the mine, stone from the quarry, arrows, fish, a tithe of emeralds from the chapel. Produce stance adds half again; Attack stance costs a quarter. A fief that's been raided produces nothing until it recovers. Kill a house's commoners and the house remembers it. *Expand* on the War Table spends 32 bread and 8 iron on one more building. It works on castles already in your world, not just new chunks.

**Inside the walls.** A castle is a programme, not a bag of rooms. The generator hands out jobs by position: the kitchen next to the hall with a hearth wall, ovens and a chimney breast; the cellar under it, casks in ranks; guardrooms by the gate with a cot, a brazier and a dice table; the barracks with bunks; the armoury beside them with racks and a grindstone; the chapel up a tower with a window, an altar and pews; the lord's solar at the top of the keep with a four-poster, a desk by the window and the house colours on the walls; a library of shelves two high; a well room; and the dungeon as low and far from the gate as it gets, cells behind iron bars with straw and chains. Beyond that, a spread of stores, quarters, infirmaries, wardrobes, brewhouses, kennels, mews and scriptoria, so a castle of forty rooms isn't thirty stores. Big rooms get ceiling beams, halls a dais with the high table and a carpet runner to it. Nearly all of it holds loot: food in the stores, arms in the armoury, iron and coal at the smithy, saddles and horse armour in the stables, and the treasury in the keep.

**No two castles alike.** Every castle has a temper that colours the dressing: a *martial* house hangs racks and targets, a *rich* one carpets, tapestries and gold, a *pious* one candles, a second chapel and a scriptorium, a *grim* one soul-fire, skulls, chains and two dungeons, a *rustic* one hay, casks, kennels and a brewhouse. Each castle plan is built twice, with a different seed, temper and roof style: slate cones, tall spires, squat hats, timber hoardings (a covered fighting gallery round the tower top, with a ladder up to it), stepped pyramids on square towers, or open crenellated platforms. With five regional palettes on top, that's about 150 distinct looks for the three great-castle plans alone.

**Fields the size of fields.** Outside the walls the fiefs farm lots 24 to 32 long and 16 to 22 across, cut into terraces four rows deep that step down the slope behind cobbled retaining walls, a water channel every few rows, a fence round the lot and a timber barn at the gate; one in three is an orchard. Inside, a castle sleeps its men: the generator counts the beds and refuses a layout with fewer bunks than the garrison it's rated for, and a castle's capacity is capped by its beds.

**The people have names.** Every soul in a fief or a castle has a name and a byname from the trade (Wat Plowman, Maud Fletcher, Hob Smith) that shows when you look at them. They keep villagers' hours: at their job block by day, in bed at night. Each field's barn has a composter and two farmers who work that field. Births and deaths go in the Chronicle by name, with who did it.

**Lived-in world.** Villagers work inside the walls, fields and orchards appear outside, and a road is laid from the gate to the nearest village as the chunks load. Castles are sited on high, dry ground and keep clear of villages and towns from any mod (anything in `#minecraft:village`, plus a few extras in `#stormhold:castle_avoid`); a watchtower or holdfast never lands inside a great castle.

## Commands

`/stormhold version` prints the commit the jar was built from; it's also in the corner of the War Table.
`/stormhold list | nearest | info | houses | realm | chronicle | matters | find <city|castle|holdfast|watchtower|town|nether>` for everyone (`find` names the nearest one already standing and where the next will stand, from the placement maths, in under a few seconds and without loading a chunk; use it instead of `/locate`, which generates structure starts chunk by chunk); `capture | raid | killlord | befriend` for ops (testing).

## Building
CI builds on every push to `main` and runs headless game tests. The jar lands on the `dist` branch, logs on `ci-logs`. Castle structures come from `tools/castlegen` (`python3 build_all.py`).
